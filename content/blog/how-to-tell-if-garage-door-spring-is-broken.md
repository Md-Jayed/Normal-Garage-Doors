When your garage door suddenly stops opening or heavy resistance prevents the motor from lifting it, a **broken garage door spring** is the single most common culprit. Garage door springs carry the full weight of your overhead door—often between 150 and 300 pounds—making them under immense, high-tension energy at all times.

Whether you live in **Normal, Bloomington**, or anywhere in McLean County, seasonal temperature fluctuations in Central Illinois put significant stress on steel springs, causing them to fatigue and snap unexpectedly.

In this guide, our master technicians explain the top 6 warning signs of a broken spring, why trying to open the door manually or automatically can cause severe damage, and the exact steps to take for fast, safe repair.

---

## 1. Loud Gunshot-Like Bang in Your Garage

Most homeowners discover a broken spring after hearing a sudden, terrifying bang coming from their attached garage—even if nobody was operating the door at that moment.

When a high-tension torsion or extension spring snapped under strain, all of its stored mechanical energy releases in a split second. If you heard a loud boom early in the morning or while resting inside your home, inspect your garage upper assembly right away.

---

## 2. A Visible 2-Inch Gap in the Overhead Spring

If your garage uses a **torsion spring system** mounted on a horizontal bar directly above the closed door opening, visual inspection is straightforward:

1. Stand inside your garage with the door completely closed.
2. Look up at the steel torsion tube mounted above the top frame.
3. Check for a distinct **2 to 3-inch gap** in the steel coil coils.

When intact, a torsion spring looks like a continuous tight coil. When broken, you will clearly see where the steel coil fractured into two separate pieces.

---

## 3. The Garage Door Only Opens 2 to 6 Inches

If you press your wall console or remote transmitter and the opener strain motor hums, lifts the door just a few inches off the concrete floor, and immediately shuts off or reverses, do not keep pressing the button!

Automatic openers are designed with safety force limits. The motor is only meant to guide the door, not lift its full unassisted dead weight. When the counterbalancing spring breaks, the opener senses extreme resistance and shuts off to prevent burning out its drive gears.

> **Warning:** Repeatedly forcing a garage door opener with a broken spring can burn out the opener motor, strip internal drive nylon gears, or bend top door panels.

---

## 4. Emergency Release Cable Feels Incredibly Heavy

When you pull the red emergency release cord to disconnect the door from the opener carriage:
- **With a good spring:** A balanced garage door can easily be lifted with one hand and will stay suspended midway up.
- **With a broken spring:** The door will feel like a solid block of lead. Attempting to lift it manually could strain your back or cause the door to crash back down violently.

If your door feels dangerously heavy, stop immediately and keep family members and pets away from the area.

---

## 5. Loose or Dangler Aircraft Cables

Torsion springs work in tandem with high-tensile steel aircraft cables wrapped around aluminum drums at each top corner of the door. When a spring snaps, tension collapses instantly, causing the side cables to slip off their winding drums and hang loosely beside the tracks.

If you notice loose, frayed, or unwound cables on either side of your door, do not attempt to rewind them yourself.

---

## 6. The Top Section of the Garage Door Is Bent or Crooked

If an opener attempts to force open a door with one broken spring (or if dual springs break unevenly), one side of the door may pull higher than the other. This uneven torque can bind the rollers inside the tracks, bend the top steel section, or pull the entire door off-track.

## Why You Should Never Attempt DIY Garage Door Spring Replacement

Garage door torsion springs store enough kinetic energy to break bones, cause severe lacerations, or knock heavy ladders over if handled without specialized winding bars and professional mechanical training.

Professional technicians use calibrated winding cones, heavy-duty vise grips, and high-cycle oil-tempered steel springs specifically matched to the exact weight, height, and wire gauge of your garage door.

## What To Do Next: Safe Emergency Action Plan

If you suspect your garage door spring is broken:

1. **Keep the door closed:** Do not attempt to force it open manually or with your opener.
2. **Unplug the garage door opener:** Prevent accidental operation from remotes or smart devices.
3. **Keep pets and vehicles safe:** If your car is trapped inside, inform your service technician so they can perform an emergency manual lift during dispatch.
