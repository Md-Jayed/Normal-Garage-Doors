Winters in **Normal and McLean County** can be brutal on residential garage doors. Sub-zero temperatures, freezing rain, road salt, and ice accumulation put extra burden on torsion springs, track alignment, rubber seals, and electric openers.

Taking 15 minutes before the first winter frost to perform routine inspection and lubrication can prevent midnight lockouts, noisy operation, and expensive emergency repairs. Here are 5 essential maintenance tips every Central Illinois homeowner should follow.

---

## 1. Inspect and Replace Cracked Bottom Weatherstripping

The rubber seal along the bottom edge of your garage door keeps freezing wind, snow drifts, rodents, and moisture out of your garage. Over time, cold weather causes cheap rubber seals to crack, stiffen, and shrink.

- **Check for light gaps:** Close your garage door during daytime and look for light leaking under the bottom seal.
- **Prevent ice sticking:** Clean ice and dirt build-up from the concrete floor under the door. Apply a thin coat of silicone spray lubricant along the rubber seal so it won't freeze stuck to the driveway.

---

## 2. Lubricate All Moving Metal Components

Cold weather causes metal parts to contract and lubricants to thicken. Dry metal rollers, hinges, and steel springs generate high friction, causing squeaking or binding during operation.

- **Use lithium or silicone spray:** Never use standard WD-40 on garage door tracks or springs, as it acts as a solvent and attracts dust. Instead, spray specialized garage door lithium or silicone lubricant on torsion springs, roller bearings, metal hinges, and lock mechanisms.
- **Do not grease tracks:** Clean tracks with a damp rag to remove debris, but leave track channels free of thick grease.

---

## 3. Test the Auto-Reverse Safety Features

Freezing weather can alter track tolerances or stiffen door travel, which might trigger your opener's safety reverse system. Test both safety systems monthly:

1. **Mechanical Reverse:** Place a 2x4 wooden board flat on the floor directly under the center of the door. Press your opener button to close the door. When the bottom edge contacts the wood, the door should immediately reverse direction.
2. **Photo-Eye Sensors:** While the door is closing, wave an object (like a broom handle) between the infrared sensors mounted 6 inches above the floor. The door should immediately stop and reverse.

---

## 4. Tighten All Track and Hinge Hardware

A standard garage door opens and closes over 1,000 times per year. All that vibration slowly loosens track bolts, roller brackets, and hinge screws.

Inspect the metal brackets holding the vertical and horizontal tracks to the wall and ceiling framing. Use a socket wrench to snug up any loose nuts or bolts. Be careful not to overtighten into wooden jambs.

---

## 5. Clear Snow and Ice From Sensor Lenses and Tracks

Blowing snow and ice buildup around your driveway entrance can easily block the photo-eye safety sensors located near the bottom corners of the opening. If your opener light blinks and the door refuses to close, check for frost or dirt on the optical sensor lenses.

Gently wipe sensor lenses with a soft microfiber cloth and clear snow drifts away from the track bases.
